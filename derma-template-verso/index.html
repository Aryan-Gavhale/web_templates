From the consulting rooms · Carlton

Two in three Australians will be told they have skin cancer. Most of it is found later than it needed to be.

We have the highest rate of skin cancer in the world and the best survival figures for it, and both facts have the same cause: this is a disease you can see. The whole of dermatology, in this country, turns on whether somebody looked.

By Dr Amrita Shroff, FACD · Dermatologist

A melanoma the width of this full stop is a fifteen-minute procedure under local anaesthetic and a scar you will forget about. The same melanoma, eighteen months later, is a conversation about lymph nodes. Nothing in between those two appointments requires cleverness. It requires somebody with a dermatoscope and forty minutes looking at all of your skin, including the parts you cannot see and the parts you would rather not show anybody.

This clinic was opened in 2009 to do that one thing properly and then to keep doing it. There is a single dermatologist. She performs every examination and every excision herself, sends every specimen to histopathology, and photographs every lesion she does not excise so that the next appointment is a comparison rather than a fresh guess.

The rest of the work follows from the same habit of looking carefully: acne that has been mismanaged for years, eczema that nobody has patch-tested, psoriasis, rosacea, hair loss, paediatric skin. Medical dermatology, in other words. We do not do cosmetic injectables, and we will tell you plainly when the thing you are worried about is nothing at all — which, more often than not, it is.

What we ask in return is that you come before you are certain. The commonest sentence in this room is I thought I was probably wasting your time, and it is said, almost without exception, by the people who were not.

The fees are printed on page six in full, with the rebate beside each one. The self-examination on page three takes five minutes a month and is the most useful thing anybody has ever been taught in this room; it is set out to be cut out and kept by a mirror.

Page seven carries the coupon. If what has brought you here is bleeding, growing or newly dark, put the coupon down and use the telephone instead — two appointments are held at eight o'clock every weekday morning and they are not given to anybody who booked online.

The six clinics

Each runs on a fixed day, so the room, the instruments and the assisting nurse are the right ones. Referral is welcome but not required.

Monday & Thursday

Skin cancer & mole mapping

Forty-five minutes, every square inch, under polarised light with a dermatoscope. Anything equivocal is photographed at fixed magnification and re-measured against its own image at the next visit rather than against memory.

Dermoscopy · baseline mapping · short-interval review

Tuesday

Surgery & histopathology

Excisions, shave and punch biopsies, and flap repairs on the face done in the rooms under local anaesthetic. Every specimen goes to the laboratory. Results are telephoned, never posted, within five working days.

Excision · biopsy · repair · suture removal

Wednesday morning

Acne & isotretinoin

Graded on the day and treated to a written schedule with an end date. Isotretinoin is prescribed where it is warranted and monitored monthly with bloods, rather than held back for years as a threat.

Topicals · oral courses · scar prevention

Wednesday afternoon

Eczema, patch testing & allergy

Chronic dermatitis that has not settled gets patch-tested rather than re-creamed. The standard series plus occupational allergens, read at 48 and 96 hours, with a written avoidance list at the end of it.

Patch testing · barrier repair · occupational skin

Friday morning

Psoriasis, rosacea & phototherapy

Narrowband UVB in the rooms, biologics where criteria are met and the paperwork done here rather than sent home with you. Severity scored at every visit so a change is a number and not an impression.

Narrowband UVB · systemics · biologics

Friday afternoon

Paediatric & adolescent skin

Birthmarks, infantile haemangiomas, molluscum, adolescent acne and the vast category of rashes that turn out to be nothing. Longer appointments, no waiting room, parents in the room throughout.

Infant · child · adolescent

Urgent lesions are not made to wait for the right day. Two appointments are held at 8am each weekday and released to nobody. Telephone for them.

Page Three

What a full skin examination actually involves

Written out in full, because most people arrive not knowing, and because the not knowing is what keeps them away.

i

You undress to your underwear

There is a gown and there is a screen, and the door is locked. A chaperone is offered every time and you may bring your own. This is the part people dread and it lasts about ninety seconds.1

1. Melanoma is found on the back more often than anywhere else in men, and on the leg in women. Both are places you cannot properly see.

ii

Every surface is looked at, in order

Scalp with a comb, behind the ears, the eyelid margins, between the toes, the soles, the nail beds, the palms, and the areas under clothing. The order is always the same so that nothing is skipped when the conversation is interesting.2

2. Acral and subungual melanoma present on palms, soles and under nails, and are the commonest presentation in darker skin — where they are also the most frequently missed.

iii

Anything of interest goes under the dermatoscope

Ten-times magnification with cross-polarised light, which shows the pigment network below the surface. Most of what looks alarming to the naked eye resolves instantly into something benign, and occasionally something flat and unremarkable does not.

iv

Photographs, at fixed distance

Lesions that do not warrant excision today are photographed with a scale in frame. At the next visit the comparison is between two images rather than between an image and a recollection.3

3. Change over time is the single most useful sign in dermoscopy. It is also the one that cannot be assessed at a first appointment, which is why the second appointment matters.

v

You leave with a written plan

One page: what was found, what was photographed, what was taken, what it costs, and the date of the next examination. A copy goes to your general practitioner unless you ask us not to send it.

To keep by the mirror

The five-minute self-examination

Once a month, in good light, after a shower. You are not looking for cancer. You are looking for the one spot that does not match the others.

Nothing ticked.

One tick is enough to make an appointment. Do not wait until you have five, and do not let anybody tell you a flat spot cannot be anything.

Letters

Printed with permission and signed as the writers asked to be signed. Nothing here has been edited except for length.

To the Editor — I put off the appointment for two years because I did not want to take my clothes off in front of a stranger. It took four minutes. The thing on my shoulder that I had been watching turned out to be nothing whatsoever, and a flat brown mark on my back that I had never seen in my life was cut out the following Tuesday. I am told it was an early melanoma. I would like the two years back but I will settle for the outcome.

— M.T., Brunswick East

To the Editor — Nine years of being told my acne was hormonal and that I should relax. Graded on the first visit here, on isotretinoin within a fortnight, monitored every month, and finished in nine. Nobody sold me a facial. I did not know that was an option.

— J.R., Coburg

To the Editor — My daughter's eczema had been treated with eleven different creams. It was patch-tested here and turned out to be an allergy to a preservative in nine of them. We were given a printed list of what to avoid and the problem was largely over in a month. Eleven creams.

— A.N., Fitzroy North

To the Editor — I was told there was nothing wrong with me and charged the consultation fee anyway, which I mention because I was braced for the opposite. On the way out I was booked in for twelve months' time. That is the entire review.

— D.W., Carlton North

Dr Amrita Shroff

A referral from your general practitioner increases your rebate and lasts twelve months. You may be seen without one; you will simply pay the full fee.
