The practice

A practice, not a chain.

Three families have run this building on Marlow Row and never changed the thing that matters: you see the same surgeon each time, and you are told the cost before anything is done. Arthur Ashbourne wrote his fees on a board by the door in 1908 because he thought it was rude not to. The board is still there.

We are eleven people. A principal dentist, two associates, an oral surgeon who comes in on Tuesdays and Thursdays, three hygienists, a technician in the laboratory downstairs, and three at the desk who will know your voice by the second call.

Nothing here is sold to you. If a tooth can be watched rather than drilled, we will tell you so and write down when to come back.

    Questions

    Asked at the desk, most weeks.

    Are you taking new patients?

    Yes, for private care, and for children under eighteen on the NHS. Adult NHS lists have been closed since 2021 and we would rather say so than keep a waiting list that never moves.

    Can I pay in instalments?

    Over six or twelve months, interest free, on anything above £400. It is arranged at the desk in about ten minutes and there is no charge for setting it up.

    I have not been for years.

    Then you are in good company, and nobody here will say a word about it. The first appointment is a look and a conversation. We will not begin treatment on the day unless you ask us to.

    Do you see children?

    From two years old, free of charge until twelve. The first visit is a ride in the chair and a look with a mirror, nothing more.

    What if I am frightened?

    Say so when you ring and it will be written at the top of your notes. You may come and sit in the room without treatment as often as you need. Sedation is available for anything long.

    Is the laboratory really here?

    In the basement, and has been since 1951. It means a crown can be fitted and adjusted the same week, and that the technician can come up and look at your face in daylight.
