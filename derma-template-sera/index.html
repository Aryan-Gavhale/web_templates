How it runs

The Séra
protocol.

Four appointments, fixed in advance. You leave the first one holding a written plan with dates on it.

  1. 01

    Consultation & mapping

    Fifty minutes. History, dermoscopic examination and full-body imaging where indicated. Photography under fixed, calibrated light so that week twelve is comparable to week zero.

    • 50 min
    • Dermoscopy
    • Baseline imaging
  2. 02

    Diagnosis in writing

    Histology and bloods return within five working days. You receive the report itself, not a summary of it, alongside the plan, the expected timeline and the point at which we change course if it isn't working.

    • 5 days
    • Full report
    • Costed plan
  3. 03

    Treatment

    Delivered in-clinic by the consultant, or dispensed with a titration schedule you can actually follow. Procedures are booked in a single block so you are not returning six times for one course.

    • Doctor-delivered
    • Blocked sessions
  4. 04

    Review at twelve weeks

    Imaging repeated under the same light and compared side by side. Either the plan continues, or it is rewritten — and we say which, in the room, with the two photographs open in front of us.

    • Included
    • Side-by-side
    • Written outcome

The practice

One consultant,
start to finish.

Séra was opened in 2009 on a narrow premise: that a skin problem is a medical problem, and that the person who diagnoses it should be the person who treats it. Every patient here is under Dr Vance's name from the first dermoscope to the last review.

Trained at UCL and the Mayo Clinic, she sits on the specialist register of the GMC and holds fellowship of the American Academy of Dermatology. The clinic keeps its list deliberately short — around fourteen patients a day — so that fifty minutes means fifty minutes.
