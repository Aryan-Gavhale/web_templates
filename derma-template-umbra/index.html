    01

    Is this mole normal?

    Almost certainly — and you were still right to ask.

    Most adults carry between twenty and forty moles and very nearly all of them are unremarkable for life.

    What we look for is not ugliness. Plenty of perfectly benign moles are large, raised and dark. What matters is difference and change: the one that does not look like the rest of yours, and the one that is not the same as it was last year. Fifteen minutes and a dermatoscope settles almost all of it on the day.

    The sentence we hear most often in this room is I thought I was probably wasting your time, and it is said, almost without exception, by the people who were not.

    02

    Will you tell me if it is cancer?

    Yes. By telephone, within five working days.

    No letters, and nothing held back until an appointment three weeks away.

    When a specimen goes to the laboratory you are told the same day when it will be back and who is going to telephone you. If the answer is one nobody wants, it comes from Dra. Bettencourt rather than from reception, and it comes with an appointment already booked in the same week.

    You are also told when the answer is boring, which it usually is. Silence is not a result and we do not use it as one.

    03

    Why has my acne come back at thirty-four?

    Because adult acne is not teenage acne arriving late.

    It is a different distribution, a different mechanism, and it wants different treatment.

    Adult acne sits lower — along the jaw, the chin and the neck — and it is more inflammatory and less comedonal than the version you had at sixteen. Which is why running the sixteen-year-old's regimen at it has not shifted anything, and why buying stronger versions of the same thing tends to make the barrier worse without touching the acne.

    Hormonal drivers get investigated rather than assumed, treatment is written down with a date at which we judge it, and where isotretinoin is genuinely warranted it is started and monitored rather than held in reserve for another three years.

    04

    How long is this going to take?

    Longer than you want. Twelve weeks before we judge anything.

    Skin turns over slowly, and almost every treatment needs a full cycle before a verdict on it is fair.

    Acne
    Twelve weeks to assess. Six months for a real answer.
    Isotretinoin
    Five to seven months, monitored monthly.
    Eczema flare
    Two weeks. If not, the diagnosis gets revisited.
    Psoriasis, systemic
    Twelve to sixteen weeks to steady state.
    A scar
    Twelve to eighteen months of remodelling.
    Mole surveillance
    Indefinitely, and that is a good outcome.

    The schedule is written down at the first visit so that you are measuring progress against a plan instead of against a hope. If a date passes and nothing has happened, that is information, and it changes the treatment rather than extending it.

    05

    Is it going to scar?

    Probably a great deal less than you are imagining.

    The scar you see at six weeks is not the scar you will have at a year.

    Redness, firmness and a raised edge are what remodelling looks like on the way through, and most of it settles without anybody doing anything. Judging a scar early is the commonest reason people spend money on treatments they did not need.

    What genuinely helps is unglamorous: keeping the sun off it for the first year, silicone if it is thickening, time, and not picking. What does not help is most of what is marketed for it. We will tell you which of the two you are holding.

    06

    Do I actually need sunscreen in winter?

    In Lisbon, yes — but not the version you have been sold.

    The UV index here clears 3 for roughly ten months of the year, and 3 is the point at which it begins to matter.

    That is not an argument for being frightened of daylight, and we will not make one. It is an argument for shade between eleven and four, a hat that actually has a brim, and sunscreen on the three places that take the damage: the face, the ears and the backs of the hands. Reapplication matters more than the number on the bottle above about factor 30.

    Behind window glass, UVB is largely stopped and UVA is not — which is why the driver's side of a face ages differently. Vitamin D is a real consideration and a solvable one; it is not a reason to burn.

    07

    Is it contagious?

    Most of what walks through this door is not.

    Eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, acne, urticaria and the large majority of rashes belong to you alone.

    A handful of things are transmissible — molluscum, scabies, impetigo, fungal infections, warts — and where that is the answer you get it plainly and in writing, with what the household needs to do and for how long, and whether a child needs to stay off school. Usually they do not.

    Nobody should be standing at the school gate guessing at this, and no parent should be treating a whole family on the strength of a search engine.

    08

    Can you just look at a photograph?

    For some things. Never for a mole.

    A photograph is genuinely useful for a rash that already has a diagnosis, for a follow-up, and for deciding how quickly you need to be seen.

    It is not adequate for a pigmented lesion. The thing that decides that answer is the dermatoscopic structure under the surface, and a telephone camera cannot see it — no matter how good the telephone is, and no matter how well lit the bathroom was.

    So if you send a photograph of a mole you will be told to come in. That is not a technique for selling you an appointment; it is the only honest answer, and saying otherwise is how lesions get missed for eighteen months.

    09

    Will you try to sell me something?

    There is nothing here to sell you.

    No injectables, no laser packages, no skincare range, no course of anything paid for in advance.

    This is a medical dermatology clinic and the only thing it charges for is a consultation and the procedures that follow from one. If a treatment is not the answer to a medical question, it is not offered here — which also means the advice you get is not downstream of a shelf that needs clearing.

    Quite often the answer is leave it alone. You will be charged for the consultation and not for a plan, and you will be told how long to leave it alone for and what would change that.

    10

    What if I cannot afford it?

    Say so when you telephone. It is not a difficult conversation.

    Every price is printed below in full, including the ones people usually have to ask for.

    Where a subsistema or a seguro de saúde covers the consultation we invoice them directly and you pay the co-payment only. Where it does not, and the money is what is stopping you, tell reception when you ring.

    Nobody should skip a skin examination over ninety euros, and this clinic would rather have the awkward conversation at the beginning than see the consequence of it four years later.
